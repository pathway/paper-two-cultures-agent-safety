# Breiman 2001, Statistical Modeling: The Two Cultures

Source: *Statistical Science* 16(3), 199–231, with comments and a rejoinder. Full text: `045_fulltext/breiman2001.txt`.
Role in this paper: the frame. Breiman's two cultures are mapped onto two cultures of agent safety; his discussants supply the limits of the analogy.

Key findings
- The data-modeling culture begins by assuming a stochastic model of the inside of the box (b2) and validates by goodness-of-fit (b9); Breiman puts 98% of statisticians in it (b4).
- The algorithmic culture treats the inside of the box as complex and unknown (b10) and validates by predictive accuracy (b11); 2% (b12).
- Breiman's central objection: conclusions drawn from a fitted model are about the model's mechanism, not nature's (b3).
- Cox: much prediction is under conditions different from the data (b6). Efron: prediction alone is only occasionally sufficient (b7). Hoadley: Fair, Isaac ran black-box scorecards under score-engineering constraints demanded by clients, law and robustness (b8).
- The Rashomon Effect: many functions give about the same error rate (b5).
